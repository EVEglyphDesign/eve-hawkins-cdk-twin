# Lane B — Open Standards and Code Sets for the Dealer-Owned Twin

**Five-line summary.** Two genuinely open, permissively-licensed, actively-maintained candidates surfaced: STAR's XML BODs (Eclipse Public License 1.0) and COVESA's Vehicle Signal Specification (MPL-2.0, v5.1, July 2025), the latter explicitly covering commercial vehicles. VMRS, ACES/PIES, and SAE J1939/J1979 are real, alive, and heavily adopted, but every one of them is a paid-subscription code set or standard, not an open license — VMRS confirmed used by PACCAR/Peterbilt dealers specifically. STAR itself is car-retail-oriented in its own language with no direct evidence of heavy-truck scope or of CDK Global membership. ADF (auto-lead XML) is a real, dead-since-2000 spec with a failed 2018 revival attempt; "OpenTrack" in the Cox Automotive/Dealertrack context is a proprietary, certification-gated integration program, not an open standard, and a same-named container-tracking product is an unrelated proprietary SaaS. FMS/rFMS (ACEA truck OEM consortium) is free-to-download and heavy-truck-native but is a data feed spec, not a licence for redistribution or a repository to fork.

## 1. STAR (Standards for Technology in Automotive Retail)

**Maintainer:** STAR, a dues-funded nonprofit standards association, incorporated 2001 ([starstandard.org](https://www.starstandard.org/)). **Alive:** yes — STAR XML 6.2.4 shipped September 12, 2024 ([STAR press release](https://www.starstandard.org/index.php/2024/09/12/leading-automotive-technical-standards-association-star-releases-star-xml-bod-updates-for-retail-delivery-vehicle-sales-pricing-and-service/)), and a new "Automotive Retail Domain Model" was released January 27, 2026 ([STAR domain model announcement](https://www.starstandard.org/index.php/2026/01/27/star-unveils-industry-defining-retail-automotive-domain-model-to-advance-data-interoperability-and-ai-transformation-across-the-entire-ecosystem/)).

**What it covers.** A Business Object Document (BOD) model — Application Area + Data Area + Verb + Noun — built on OAGIS/UN-CEFACT CCTS methodology and W3C XML Schema ([STAR BOD guideline docs](https://docs.starstandard.org/guidelines6/STAR614/BOD/)). Per the [official BOD catalogue](https://www.starstandard.org/index.php/star-xml-bods/), transactions span General (Standard Codes, Confirm), Customer (Credit Application/Contract/Decision, Customer Information, Sales Lead, Service Appointment), Dealer (Financial Metrics/Statement), Parts Management, Vehicle Management & Sales (Inventory, Invoice, Order, Specifications, Remarketing), and Vehicle Repair & Service (Repair Order, Service Plan, Vehicle Service History) — over 200 XML message formats across 35+ business areas. The April 2023 STAR6 update added Vehicle Telematics, Sales Lead, Credit Decision, and Customer Information nouns, bringing the BOD count to 54 ([Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/autofinjournal/star-releases-sales-process-standards-api-updated-star6-xml/)). The January 2026 Domain Model is viewable by the whole industry regardless of membership, though only members can edit it; finalized domains are Parts, Accounts Payable, Accounting, Payroll, and HR ([STAR press release](https://www.starstandard.org/index.php/2026/01/27/star-unveils-industry-defining-retail-automotive-domain-model-to-advance-data-interoperability-and-ai-transformation-across-the-entire-ecosystem/)).

**Licence.** At least one published STAR schema states explicitly: "This schema is made available under an Eclipse Public Licenses 1.0" ([Liquid Technologies STAR schema documentation](https://schemas.liquid-technologies.com/STAR/5.3.4/processrepairorder_xsd.html)) — a genuine, permissive open-source licence, distinct from every other candidate in this report.

**Cost of membership** (separate from the schema licence — membership buys voting rights and early/full document access): dealers $10,000–$20,000/year tiered by rooftop/fleet count; manufacturers $10,000–$50,000 tiered by revenue; IT vendors $2,500–$40,000 tiered by dealers served; national dealer associations $25,000; NADA-affiliated associations $100 flat ([STAR membership application PDF](https://www.starstandard.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/STAR-MEMBERSHIP-APPLICATION.pdf); [membership types page](https://www.starstandard.org/index.php/membership-types/)).

**Heavy truck coverage: not established — inference only.** STAR's own materials describe "vehicles" and "automobiles" generically; no STAR primary source reviewed states explicit Class 8/heavy-truck dealership scope. This is an absence of evidence, not a confirmed exclusion, and it should be recorded as a gap rather than papered over.

**DMS conformance, including CDK.** Reynolds and Reynolds joined STAR in 2022 ([Reynolds and Reynolds press release](https://www.reyrey.com/company/media-center/news-releases/reynolds-and-reynolds-help-set-data-standards-automotive-retail)) and DealerBuilt joined in 2026, naming its "iService Fixed Operations Platform" in the same release, which confirms "Fixed Operations" as live vendor-ecosystem terminology ([PRWeb, DealerBuilt/STAR](https://www.prweb.com/releases/dealerbuilt-joins-leading-automotive-technical-standards-association-star-302674790.html)). **No primary source found confirms CDK Global as a STAR member.** CDK's own [Network Protect product page](https://www.cdkglobal.com/dealership-operations/cybersecurity/cdk-network-protect) states its controls "address GLBA, Star Standards and NIST Standards for data security" — this is a cybersecurity-compliance claim only, not evidence of BOD technical conformance, and the two must not be conflated. Salesforce Automotive Cloud does implement STAR BODs as downloadable integration resources (Repair Order, Vehicle Inventory, Sales Lead, Customer Information) ([Salesforce STAR integration docs](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/industries/automotive/guide/star-integrations.html)), demonstrating real non-dealer-OEM adoption.

## 2. VMRS (Vehicle Maintenance Reporting Standards)

**Maintainer:** American Trucking Associations since 1970; custodianship held by the Technology & Maintenance Council (TMC) of ATA since 1997. Current edition VMRS 2.0 ([TMC VMRS Overview](https://tmc.trucking.org/VMRS-Overview); [TMC VMRS Introduction Handbook](https://tmc.trucking.org/sites/default/files/VMRS_INTRO.pdf)). **Alive:** yes, actively licensed and updated on a subscription cycle; pricing/terms confirmed current via [TMC's 2021 licensing flyer](https://tmc.trucking.org/sites/default/files/VMRS_flyer_2021_web.pdf) and an [annual-subscription move reported by Transport Topics](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/tmc-updates-vmrs-licenses-moves-annual-subscription-0).

**What it covers.** A hierarchical code-key system for maintenance and equipment: Code Key 1 (Equipment Vocation), 14 (Reason for Repair), 15 (Work Accomplished), 18 (Technician Failure), 31/32/33 (System/Assembly/Component, three-digit each — 34,000+ component codes), 34 (Manufacturer/Supplier/Brand ID, 11,000+ identifiers), plus tire-position, maintenance-status, and warranty-claim keys — roughly 58 active code keys of a possible 64–84. VMRS supplements, and explicitly does not replace, OEM part numbers.

**Licence and cost — not free at any tier.** Four paid annual products: Electronic Catalog ($350–$2,250/yr by revenue, Code Keys 31–34 only), Complete Corporate ($1,000–$6,250/yr, internal use), Complete Developer ($1,500/$2,250 flat, no redistribution rights), and Complete Distribution ($6,000–$51,000/yr by user tier, includes SaaS/software distribution rights) ([TMC VMRS pricing flyer](https://tmc.trucking.org/sites/default/files/VMRS_flyer_2021_web.pdf)). This stands in direct contrast to STAR's Eclipse Public License.

**Mandate status.** No legal or government mandate found. VMRS's own handbook frames adherence as an internal-standard requirement for *external* reporting/data interchange, not a statutory one.

**Heavy truck / PACCAR-Peterbilt use: confirmed.** PACCAR Parts Fleet Services' ECAT system explicitly uses VMRS codes for Kenworth and Peterbilt dealers, per PACCAR's own marketing manager quoted in [Trailer Body Builders](https://www.trailer-bodybuilders.com/equipment-parts/aftermarket-parts/article/21732413/paccar-parts-fleet-services-enhances-electronic-catalog). PACCAR's Decisiv-based service portal has dedicated "VMRS CODE" search fields ([PACCAR Solutions support docs](https://support.paccar.decisiv.net/hc/en-us/articles/360026414773-Use-the-Search-Page)), and "Peterbilt of Alaska" appears directly on TMC's licensee list alongside Daimler Trucks North America ([TMC licensee announcement](https://tmc.trucking.org/blog/tmc-announces-news-licensees-latest-vmrs)). VMRS is the clearest fit for the group's heavy-truck side of any candidate reviewed — but it is licensed code, not an open schema.

## 3. ATA TMC Recommended Practices

**Maintainer:** Technology & Maintenance Council of ATA. Confirmed live via the [TMC RP Manual description index](https://tmc.trucking.org/sites/default/files/RP_MANUAL_DESCRIPTIONS.pdf) and [TMC Recommended Practices page](https://tmc.trucking.org/TMC-Recommended-Practices).

**What it covers, relevant to service-event data:**
- **RP 1210C** — "Windows Application Program Interface," a vendor-neutral API standard between a physical datalink (CAN/J1939), a Vehicle Diagnostic Adapter, and Windows software, first published 1997 ([Transport Topics history](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/data-interchangeinteroperability-challenges-carriers-take-action)). Confirmed OEM-independent and widely implemented by third parties including Noregon JPRO, Vector, and Softing ([Softing RP1210 overview](https://automotive.softing.com/standards/programming-interfaces/rp1210.html)).
- **RP 1612** — Service Event Data Transparency: defines the electronic interface between third-party maintenance providers and commercial vehicle owners for consistent repair-status communication — directly on-point for a service-event data model.
- **RP 1602** — Repair Order Authorization and Approval workflow templates.
- **RP 1603** — Warranty Workflow Procedures.
- **RP 1214A** — Event Recording: Collection, Storage & Retrieval.
- **RP 1216** — Vehicle to Office Data Communications.
- **RP 1226** — Vehicle Accessory Connector Guidelines (non-OEM-specific aftermarket connector).
- **RP 2203** — Trailer Tracking Interface Standard.

**Gap.** No explicit non-member download price was found for individual RPs; TMC membership costs seen elsewhere (roughly $850–$1,400 for annual meeting registration) relate to conference participation, not confirmed RP-document licensing terms. This should be treated as an open question, not resolved by inference.

## 4. ACES and PIES (Auto Care Association)

**Maintainer:** Auto Care Association (nonprofit trade association), via industry workgroups and its Technology Standards Committee ([autocare.org/aces](https://www.autocare.org/aces); [autocare.org/pies](https://www.autocare.org/pies)). **Alive:** ACES 5.0 and PIES 8.0 both released April 2, 2026, per the [Auto Care Association's own release](https://www.autocare.org/news/latest-news/details/2026/04/02/auto-care-association-releases-aces--5.0-and-pies--8.0), superseding an earlier ACES 4.2/PIES 7.2 baseline reported by a secondary trade source.

**What it covers.** ACES (Aftermarket Catalog Exchange Standard) is fitment/compatibility data (year/make/model), and PIES (Product Information Exchange Standard) is product-attribute/content data; both are XML-based and rely on shared reference databases (VCdb, Qdb, PCdb, PAdb, Brand Table). Critically, Auto Care Association states directly that both standards cover **"light, medium, and heavy duty, as well as powersport, off-highway and equipment"** — a genuine match to both halves of the Hawkins group, since it spans heavy truck (Peterbilt Atlantic) and powersports (Extreme Torque Motorsports). A separate VCdb subscription tier exists specifically for medium- and heavy-duty trucks (GVW classes 4–8).

**Licence and cost — not free.** Annual paid subscription required for database access. The Entry Level Package (companies under $1M global revenue) is $2,500/year (member price) and includes Light Duty, Powersports, Medium Duty, Heavy Duty, and Off-Highway VCdb plus Qdb/PCdb/PAdb/Brand Table access ([Auto Care Association subscription page](https://www.autocare.org/data-standards/subscriptions)); non-members pay roughly 40% more. The XML schemas themselves are machine-readable and a free validator tool (Catalog Assessment Reporting Tool) exists on AutoCareVIP.com, but the underlying reference data is gated behind the subscription.

## 5. ADF (Auto-Lead Data Format), successors, OpenTrack, Auto-ISAC

**ADF.** A real, open, XML-based lead-exchange spec, version 1.0 finalized May 2000 by a consortium of thirteen automotive internet companies, hosted at [adfxml.info](https://adfxml.info/adf_spec.pdf). It defines five categories — lead/prospect, vehicle, customer, vendor, service provider. **Not alive in any meaningful sense**: the specification has not been revised since 2000. A 2018 industry effort to publish "ADF 2.0," backed by 17 vendors including Cox Automotive, DealerSocket, and Edmunds, was announced with intent to add chat-transcript and service/parts-lead elements ([PR Newswire, ADF 2.0 announcement](https://www.prweb.com/releases/auto_industry_to_endorse_adf_2_0_specifications/prweb15347462.htm)), but no finished, published ADF 2.0 specification was found at any primary source — this is an absence of evidence, and ADF 1.0 remains the only confirmed-published version. **Inference:** a recently surfaced "Automotive Commerce Protocol (ACP)," self-described by a single named individual as a 2026 replacement for ADF, could not be verified as an industry-adopted standard from any independent primary source and should be treated as an unverified personal initiative, not an established standard.

**OpenTrack — not an open standard.** In the Cox Automotive/Dealertrack context, "Opentrack" is the proprietary, certification-gated third-party integration program for the Dealertrack DMS: vendors must sign an integration agreement and be certified "at Dealertrack's sole discretion," with confidential specifications ([Dealertrack Opentrack Integration Agreement PDF](https://www.coxautoinc.com/terms/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/Dealertrack-Open-track-Integration-Agreement-v4-10-16-19.pdf); [Supergood Systems API assessment](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/dealertrack-dms)). There is no public developer portal or open credential issuance. Separately, a same-named "OpenTrack" product at opentrack.co is an unrelated proprietary container/shipping-logistics visibility SaaS with no connection to automotive retail ([OpenTrack FAQ](https://www.opentrack.co/faq)) — a naming collision, not the same standard, and this must not be conflated with the Dealertrack program.

**Auto-ISAC.** A real, active, industry-operated cybersecurity information-sharing hub, formed 2015, explicitly covering "light- and heavy-duty vehicle OEMs, suppliers and the commercial vehicle sector" ([Automotive ISAC](https://automotiveisac.com/)). It is a threat-intelligence sharing community, not a data-schema or interoperability standard — membership grants access to a shared threat portal, not a lead, service, or transaction data format. Not directly relevant to twin data modeling, though relevant to the twin's security posture if PACCAR or Hawkins group entities are Auto-ISAC members (membership not verified here).

## 6. Telematics standards: FMS Standard, rFMS, SAE J1939/J1979, TMC RPs

**FMS Standard.** Developed 2002 by Mercedes-Benz, MAN, Scania, Volvo/Renault, DAF, and Iveco under ACEA's Heavy Truck Electronic Interface working group; free to download after registration, no fee, at [fms-standard.com](https://www.fms-standard.com/Truck/down_load/download_ok.htm). **Alive:** version 05 updated November 11, 2025. Broadcasts a defined telemetry set (speed, fuel used/level, RPM, axle weight, engine hours, VIN, tachograph data, coolant temperature) coded to SAE J1939, at repetition rates from 20ms to 10s ([Wikipedia, FMS-Standard, cross-checked against the primary fms-standard.com technical description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FMS-Standard); [FMS-Standard v05 technical document](https://www.fms-standard.com/Truck/down_load/fms%20document_v_05_vers.07.07.2024.pdf)). Explicitly heavy-truck- and bus-native — the opposite scope emphasis from STAR.

**rFMS (remote FMS).** The same OEM consortium's cloud/API successor, allowing vehicle data retrieval directly from OEM servers without extra hardware; current version 5.0.0 released July 25, 2025, implemented as a RESTful HTTPS API with new push-based delivery in addition to the original pull method ([rFMS v5.0.0 technical specification](https://www.fms-standard.com/Truck/down_load/Technical_Specification_rFMS_vehicle_data_V5.0.0_25.07.2025.pdf)). ACEA's own position papers describe FMS and rFMS as "open standards for a common interface for commercial vehicle data," coded under SAE J1939 ([ACEA position paper on HDV data access](https://www.acea.auto/files/ACEA-position-paper-access-to-data-functions-and-resources-for-the-HDV-market.pdf)). Free to access; not an open licence for redistribution, and it does not define the transport link from OEM server to the twin — that remains OEM-specific.

**SAE J1939 / J1979.** J1939 defines the CAN-based application and data-link layers underlying both VMRS-adjacent diagnostics and FMS/rFMS; J1979 defines OBD parameter IDs for vehicle data requests ([Astra Telematics CANBus compatibility note, cross-referencing both standards](https://astratelematics.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/appnote_canbus_compatibility.pdf)). **Not free** — SAE International sells J1939 access via a paid annual subscription package (SAE's own [J1939 Standards Collection page](https://www.sae.org/publications/collections/content/j1939_dl/) and [small-business J1939 subscription page](https://saemobilus.org/who-we-serve/corporations/small-businesses/)); individual documents are also sold separately by resellers (e.g., $683.30 for the J1939 Digital Annex via [Intertek Inform](https://www.intertekinform.com/en-au/standards/sae-j1939da-202412-1020775_saig_sae_sae_3515211/)). No free tier found.

**TMC telematics-specific RPs.** Beyond RP 1216 (Vehicle to Office Data Communications) and RP 2203 (Trailer Tracking), already listed under item 3, no additional TMC RP exclusively dedicated to telematics data content was identified in the RP Manual index reviewed; this is recorded as a gap rather than assumed absent.

## 7. Other open standards found: COVESA Vehicle Signal Specification (VSS)

Not on the original list but directly relevant. **Maintainer:** COVESA (Connected Vehicle Systems Alliance), successor effort to the GENIVI Alliance's original VSS work, introduced 2016 ([COVESA VSS project page](https://covesa.global/project/vehicle-signal-specification/)). **Licence: MPL-2.0**, genuinely open source, hosted at [github.com/COVESA/vehicle_signal_specification](https://github.com/COVESA/vehicle_signal_specification). **Alive:** version 5.1 released July 29, 2025, per the [GitHub releases page](https://github.com/COVESA/vehicle_signal_specification/releases/), with the design work continuing (a new "Vehicle Data Modeling" project was launched October 2025, per [COVESA's own announcement](https://covesa.global/covesa-launches-a-new-vehicle-data-modeling-project/)). **What it covers:** a hierarchical, technology-agnostic tree of vehicle signals (e.g., `Vehicle.Powertrain.CombustionEngine.RPM`) intended as a common naming layer independent of transport protocol; COVESA states explicitly it is designed to "support a robust variety of vehicles, including consumer and commercial vehicles" ([COVESA VSS overview PDF](https://covesa.global/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/COVESA-Vehicle-Signal-Specification-Enabling-Ecosystems_20240105.pdf)). VSS is a signal-naming taxonomy, not a transaction/document standard like STAR's BODs — it does not by itself cover repair orders, invoices, or parts catalogues, so it complements rather than replaces the retail-transaction standards above. A companion access-protocol layer, VISS (Vehicle Information Service Specification), is also open and COVESA-maintained ([VISS explainer, COVESA GitHub](https://github.com/COVESA/vehicle-information-service-specification/blob/main/VISS-explainer.md)). Separately, ISO 20078 (Extended Vehicle, "ExVe") defines an OAuth2-based web-services access framework referenced by ACEA alongside FMS/rFMS and COVESA ([ACEA connected-vehicle data-sharing position paper](https://www.acea.auto/files/ACEA_Position_paper_Connected_vehicle_data_sharing.pdf)), but ISO standards are sold, not open — an ISO 20078-2:2021 licence costs roughly 375 AUD from a national standards reseller ([Standards Australia store listing](https://store.standards.org.au/product/iso-20078-2-2021)), so ExVe is recorded here as real and alive but not open.

No further open equivalents to STAR, VMRS, ACES/PIES, or ADF were identified in this pass beyond COVESA VSS/VISS and ISO 20078; this absence is recorded as a finding rather than filled with speculation.
